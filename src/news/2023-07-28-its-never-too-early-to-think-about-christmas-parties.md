Starting early isn't about adding pressure. It's about keeping your options open: the date you want, the venue you'd prefer, and enough time to get the details right for everyone who'll be there. As corporate event specialists with over 15 years of experience, we see every December how much difference a few extra weeks of planning makes.

This guide covers:

- A month-by-month planning timeline.
- How to set a sensible budget.
- Choosing the right format for your team.
- Making the party work for everyone.
- What to think about when planning your entertainment.

There's also a checklist at the end that you can save and work through as you go.

## Why Christmas Party Planning Needs to Start Early

December has fewer suitable party dates than most people expect. Once you rule out weekends, the final week before Christmas and the days when half the team is on annual leave, you're usually left with a handful of Thursdays and Fridays in the first three weeks of the month. Most other organisations in the country are aiming for those same dates.

Venues feel this first. Many start taking Christmas bookings over the summer, and the most popular spaces for Friday nights in early December are often gone by the autumn. Entertainment suppliers see the same pattern. At Fun Pro UK, December Fridays and weekends are always the first dates to fill.

There's an internal side to it too. Budgets need signing off, headcounts need estimating, and if you're planning ++[branded games](https://www.funprouk.co.uk/branded-game-hire/)++ or personalised ++[photo booth](https://www.funprouk.co.uk/products/christmas-photo-booths/)++ prints, artwork needs designing and approving. All of that takes longer inside a busy organisation than you might think.

Early planning also makes your party more inclusive. Colleagues with caring responsibilities, part-time hours or long commutes can make arrangements when they have plenty of notice.

If you're reading this in October or November, don't worry. You haven't left it too late. The timeline below shows you what to prioritise, and smaller parties can comfortably work to a shorter schedule.

H2: Your Month-by-Month Christmas Party Planning Timeline

![](/images/december-calendar.png)

This timeline works back from your party date. For a large company celebration, it's worth following it from the start. For a smaller team gathering, you can compress the early stages.

### Four to six months before: set the foundations

This is where the big decisions happen, and every later choice depends on them. Speak to whoever holds the budget and agree what it needs to cover. Then pull together a shortlist of possible dates. It's worth avoiding the last week before Christmas, when annual leave and family commitments are at their peak.

- Agree the budget and what it includes.
- Shortlist two or three possible dates.
- Decide on the broad format (more on this below).
- Run a quick poll asking the team about preferred dates, format and whether they'd like alcohol to be central to the evening.
- Shortlist venues and make initial enquiries.

### Three months before: book the big pieces

With your budget and date agreed, it's time to secure the elements that are hardest to change later.

- Book your venue, checking capacity, accessibility and what's included.
- Firm up an estimated headcount.
- Start entertainment enquiries. Ask suppliers about space, power and access requirements now, as these can affect which room or venue works best.
- Decide whether partners or families are invited, as this changes the mix of activities you'll need.

### Eight to ten weeks before: confirm your entertainment

This is the window we recommend for confirming your ++[Christmas games](https://www.funprouk.co.uk/categories/christmas-game-hire/)++ and entertainment.

If you'd like ++[custom-branded games](https://www.funprouk.co.uk/branded-game-hire/)++ or photo booth prints featuring your company logo, have your artwork ready at around six to eight weeks before the event. This gives enough time for printing. Our guide to [++planning Christmas entertainment++](https://www.funprouk.co.uk/events/christmas-entertainment/) goes into more detail on the entertainment side.

- Confirm your games and entertainment booking.
- Prepare and approve any branding artwork.
- Check that your venue is happy with your entertainment plans.

### Six weeks before: invitations and guest details

Send your invitations with everything guests need to know: date, time, venue, dress code, plus-one policy and an RSVP deadline. Use the RSVP to collect dietary requirements and any accessibility needs, rather than guessing on the night.

- Send invitations with a clear RSVP date.
- Collect dietary and accessibility information.
- Plan transport home, particularly for venues outside town centres.
- Decide how remote or hybrid colleagues will be included.

### Four weeks before: logistics

This is when the practical details come together. Your suppliers will need to know how they can get into the venue, and your venue will want a clear picture of the evening.

- Confirm final numbers with your venue and caterer.
- Share access details with suppliers, including loading times, lifts, door widths, parking and the location of power sockets.
- Agree a running order covering arrivals, food, speeches or awards, entertainment and finish time.
- Let staff know what to expect, including any guidance on behaviour.

### The final week and on the day

By now, most of the work is done.

- Send a friendly reminder with the key details.
- Confirm supplier arrival times. Our team typically arrives one to two hours before your event starts to set up.
- Arrive early yourself and keep your running order to hand.
- Nominate a single point of contact for suppliers, so you get to enjoy the party too.

## How to Set a Christmas Party Budget

A per-head figure is a useful place to start. Once you know roughly how much you can spend per guest, split the total into categories:

- Venue
- Food and drink
- Entertainment
- Transport
- Decorations
- A contingency buffer for anything unexpected

Before you commit, check what's already included in each quote. Some venues include decorations or a DJ, which frees up budget elsewhere. When you hire Christmas games from us, ++[delivery](https://www.funprouk.co.uk/locations/)++, setup and collection are included, although delivery fees may apply depending on your location.

It's also worth knowing about HMRC's exemption for annual staff events. Under section 264 of the ++[Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act 2003](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2003/1/section/264)++, employers can spend up to £150 per head in a tax year on qualifying annual events, such as a Christmas party, without it counting as a taxable benefit for employees. A few points to bear in mind:

- The event generally needs to be open to all staff, or all staff at a particular location.
- The £150 includes VAT and associated costs such as transport and accommodation.
- It's a limit rather than an allowance, so going over it, even slightly, can make the whole amount taxable.

Rules can change and every organisation's circumstances are different, so check the current HMRC guidance or speak to your accountant or payroll team before finalising your spend.

One practical tip from experience: a few well-chosen activities usually deliver more than a budget spread thinly across lots of smaller things.

## Work Christmas Party Ideas: Choosing the Right Format

There's no single right way to celebrate. The best format depends on your team's size and culture, your budget, and what you'd like the party to achieve. That might be thanking people for their hard work, bringing departments together, or simply marking the end of the year.

Here are the most common options and who they tend to suit:

- Sit-down dinner: A restaurant or hotel meal works well for smaller teams and gives everyone time to talk. It can feel formal for larger groups, so think about adding something to do before or after the meal.
- Drinks and canapés reception: More relaxed and sociable, but without a focal point, conversation can stay within existing friendship groups. This is where ++[interactive entertainment](https://www.funprouk.co.uk/categories/interactive-game-hire/)++ really helps.
- In-office or on-site party: Often great value, easy for shift workers to attend, and no travel to organise. With the right decorations and entertainment, a familiar space can feel completely different for the day. Our [++office entertainment++](https://www.funprouk.co.uk/events/office-entertainment/) page has more ideas.
- Daytime or lunchtime celebration: An inclusive choice for parents, carers, non-drinkers and anyone with a long journey home. It's increasingly popular with teams who'd rather not have a late night.
- Activity-led party: Built around games, [++casino tables++](https://www.funprouk.co.uk/products/casino-table-hire/) or a festive funfair theme, this format gives everyone a shared experience and plenty to talk about.
- Shared party night or exclusive venue hire: Shared nights can be cost-effective for smaller groups. Exclusive hire gives you control over timings, music and entertainment.
- Christmas party and awards evening: Combining the two is a lovely way to recognise the year's achievements. Take a look at our ideas for [++award ceremonies++](https://www.funprouk.co.uk/events/company-award-ceremonies/).

If some of your team work remotely, think about how they can feel part of the celebration too. That might mean travel support to attend in person, or a smaller online element alongside the main event.

## Planning a Christmas Party Everyone Can Enjoy

The most common reason a work party falls flat isn't the budget. It's a plan that suits the people who organised it rather than the whole team. A little thought early on makes a real difference.

### Not everyone celebrates Christmas

Many organisations frame their party as an end-of-year celebration, which helps everyone feel welcome. It's also worth making sure attendance feels genuinely optional.

### Alcohol doesn't need to be the centrepiece

Offer a proper choice of non-alcoholic drinks, not just water and orange juice. Consider drink tokens rather than a free bar, and give people another reason to be there. When the activities are the highlight, the evening becomes more about spending time together.

### Think about quieter personalities

For many people, standing around making small talk with colleagues from other departments is the hardest part of the night. Games and activities give guests something to do and something to talk about. They also let people join in or simply watch, whichever they're more comfortable with.

### Check accessibility

Make sure your venue has step-free access, accessible toilets and enough seating. Check that music levels still allow conversation, and choose some activities that can be enjoyed seated or as a spectator.

### Plan for families if they're invited

If children are coming along, mix activities for different ages. A [++Santa's grotto++](https://www.funprouk.co.uk/categories/christmas-grotto-hire/) creates a magical focal point for younger guests, while a Christmas Prize Crane appeals to all ages.

## Christmas Party Games and Entertainment: What to Consider

![](/images/group-of-people-celebrating-christmas-party.png)

Good entertainment does more than fill time. It breaks down barriers between departments, gets people laughing together and gives colleagues a chance to see each other in a completely different light.

To get the most from your entertainment, it's worth thinking about the practical side early:

- How many games you'll need: As a rough guide, two to three games suit a party of 20 to 50 people. Four to six games create a proper entertainment area for 50 to 150 guests, and larger parties may need six or more.
- Space: Each game typically needs around 2m x 2m of floor space, plus room for people to queue and watch. An entertainment zone with four to six games needs roughly 40 to 50 square metres.
- Power: Most games run from a standard 13A socket, so check how many your venue has and where they are.
- Access: Let your supplier know about lifts, stairs, door widths and any loading restrictions.
- Noise and timing: Lively games can compete with speeches in a smaller room, so plan where they go and when they're in use.
- Variety: Mix quick solo challenges, head-to-head games and team activities, plus things people will enjoy watching.

## Keeping Your Christmas Party Safe and Professional

Most work Christmas parties run without a hitch, and a bit of preparation helps keep it that way. It's worth remembering that a work party is an extension of the workplace, so your usual policies on conduct, harassment and alcohol still apply.

A few simple steps help protect both your colleagues and you as the organiser:

- Remind staff of expectations before the event in a friendly, positive way.
- Consider a simple risk assessment for the evening, thinking about what could happen during and after the party.
- Make sure people know how to raise a concern on the night if they need to.
- Plan safe transport home, especially for late finishes.
- Check that your suppliers hold ++[public liability insurance](https://www.funprouk.co.uk/safety-and-insurance/)++ and can provide risk assessments.
