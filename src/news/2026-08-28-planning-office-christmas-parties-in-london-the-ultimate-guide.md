## Why Christmas Parties in London Need Booking Earlier Than Anywhere Else

![](/images/christmas-parties-london.png)

December Fridays and Saturdays in central London are the most heavily booked dates in the UK events calendar. Venues open bookings early, fill their prime slots first, and by autumn the good rooms on the good nights have gone.

The effect spreads further than the venue. Caterers, AV suppliers, photographers and entertainment providers are all working the same compressed diary. Book late and you are not just choosing from what is left; you are choosing from what is left across every part of your event at once.

For a December event in London, eight to ten weeks is a sensible minimum. If you need a specific date, a large capacity, or anything branded, start earlier.

It is worth considering the alternatives before you commit. Tuesday to Thursday dates in December are easier to secure, usually cost less per head, and most teams do not mind in the slightest. A January celebration is another option that more companies are taking seriously, particularly where budgets are tight and diaries in December are already impossible.

## Setting a Realistic Budget

Most organisers think in cost per head, and that figure has to stretch across venue hire, food and drink, entertainment, staffing and transport. In central London the venue takes a larger share of that than almost anywhere else in the UK, which is where budgets usually run into trouble.

Work out what the venue leaves you before you commit to it, not after.

### The £150 per head rule most organisers only half remember

HMRC allows employers to provide annual staff functions without triggering Income Tax or National Insurance, provided certain conditions are met. Three things matter:

- It must be an annual function, such as a Christmas party or summer event
- It must be open to all employees, or to all employees at a particular location or department
- The total cost must not exceed £150 per person attending, including VAT

Here is the detail that catches people out. The £150 is a threshold, not an allowance. Go over it by any amount and the entire cost becomes a taxable benefit, not just the excess.

The calculation includes everything connected to the event: food, drink, venue hire, entertainment, transport and accommodation. You divide the total by everyone who attends, including any guests employees bring.

It is also one annual figure covering all qualifying events in the tax year. If you held a summer social, part of it has already been used. That is a good reason to plan the year's staff events together rather than treating December in isolation.

Tax rules change and every company's position is different, so check yours with your accountant before you build a budget around it.

### Where London budgets tend to go wrong

- Comparing quotes without checking which include VAT
- Forgetting the cost of getting people home safely
- Booking the venue first and finding the entertainment budget has already disappeared
- Not allowing any contingency for final numbers moving

## Choosing the Right Format for Your Team

There are three broad routes, and the right one depends on your numbers, your budget and how much control you need.

### Shared party nights

Several companies attend the same venue on the same night, usually as a set package with food, drinks and entertainment included. The value per head is good and there is very little for you to organise.

The trade off is control. You cannot shape the atmosphere, the running order or anything specific to your company, and you will be sharing the room.

### Exclusive venue hire

You take the whole space. That gives you control over timings, layout, branding and the running order, which matters if you are presenting awards, marking a milestone year or inviting clients.

It costs more and asks more of you in coordination, but for corporate Christmas parties with a purpose beyond celebrating, it is usually the right call.

### Your own space

An office, a warehouse or a hired blank venue puts everything in your hands: furniture, catering, licensing, entertainment. It is more work, but it is often the most cost efficient route, and it lets you spend the budget on what people will remember rather than on the postcode.

## Your London Christmas Party Planning Timeline

Working backwards from the date makes the whole thing manageable.

### Eight to ten weeks before

Confirm your venue, guest numbers and budget. Check availability with your suppliers, particularly for December Fridays and Saturdays.

### Six to eight weeks before

Finalise your entertainment. If you want anything carrying your company ++[branding](https://www.funprouk.co.uk/categories/branded-exhibition-games/)++, artwork is needed at this point so it can go through our in house print workshop in time.

### Four weeks before

Confirm logistics. ++[Delivery](https://www.funprouk.co.uk/locations/)++ access, setup windows, power supply and any venue restrictions all need to be agreed now rather than on the day.

### One week before

Agree final timings, including arrival, setup duration and collection. We typically arrive one to two hours before your guests do.

Venues and caterers will ask for final numbers earlier than you expect, often two to three weeks out. Build that into your internal deadline for RSVPs.

## London Venue Logistics That Catch Organisers Out

This is where London events differ most from the rest of the country, and where a small oversight causes a large problem on the day.

### Loading and access

Many central London venues have restricted delivery windows, shared loading bays or no direct vehicle access at all. Ask before you book, not after.

### Lifts and stairs

Interactive equipment cannot be carried up or down flights of stairs. If your room is not on the ground floor, you need lift access, and the lift needs to fit the equipment.

### Doorways and corridors

Most of our games need a minimum door width of around 0.6m. Period buildings, converted townhouses and basement bars are where this becomes an issue.

### Floor surface

Equipment needs a level indoor surface to run safely.

### Power

Most games run from a standard 13A socket. Older venues often have fewer accessible sockets than the floor plan suggests, so check early and ask your supplier about distribution and extension leads.

### Noise

High energy games are excellent in a large room and a genuine problem next to a speech. Think about zoning the space so the two do not compete.

### Driving in

The Congestion Charge applies Monday to Friday from 7am to 6pm, and from midday to 6pm at weekends and on bank holidays, with ULEZ operating across Greater London around the clock. Check the current rates on the TfL website, as they change, and factor them in for anyone travelling by car.

### Getting home

Last trains, night tube coverage and cab availability on peak December nights are all worth checking before you fix an end time. It is one of the most common complaints after a London Christmas party, and one of the easiest to avoid.

## Making Sure Everyone Actually Enjoys It

A London office is usually a mix of departments, ages, cultures and personalities who rarely interact during the working day. Plenty of office Christmas parties in London default to a format built around a bar and a set menu, which works well for some of those people and not at all for others.

A few things are worth thinking about early:

- Colleagues who do not drink, whether for cultural, religious, health or personal reasons, need somewhere to be and something to do that is not standing near the bar
- Hybrid and remote team members travelling in need more notice, and sometimes help with travel or accommodation
- People who find large social events difficult do far better when there is something to do rather than only something to talk about
- Step free access, seating and enough room to move around any activities all matter more than they get credit for

That last point is the practical argument for activities rather than a purely social evening. Shared games give people who barely know each other a reason to interact, which is exactly what a room full of separate departments needs.
